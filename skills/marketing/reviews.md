# Reviews and testimonials — asking honestly, reusing with permission

How to ask customers for reviews and how to use what they say on a page, for apps and for small
local businesses. A review is evidence for [SKILL.md](SKILL.md)'s sixth question; this file keeps
that evidence real.

Adapted from the `referrals` and `influencer-marketing` skills in `coreyhaines31/marketingskills`
(MIT); rewritten, with the manipulative parts left out.

## The rules

1. **Ask everyone, or a random or representative set.** Use one rule for who gets asked and when,
   applied the same to happy, neutral and unhappy customers.
2. **Never filter who gets asked by sentiment.** Asking only people who gave a high score, a
   thumbs up or a 9–10 on a survey, and sending everyone else to a private feedback form, is
   review-gating. It fails the honesty check and breaks several platforms' rules. A complaint can
   start a separate support follow-up; it never removes that customer from the review ask.
3. **No incentives where the platform forbids them.** Where a platform allows a small thank-you,
   disclose it on the review and wherever the review is reused.
4. **Never tie anything to what the review says.** No reward, refund, fix or discount for a good
   rating, for changing a review, or for removing one. Disclosure does not make a sentiment
   condition acceptable.
5. **The customer writes the review.** No prewritten text, no "leave five stars", no staff or
   friends posing as customers, no AI-drafted reviews submitted in a customer's name.
6. **Reuse a review or testimonial only with permission and with its source.** See *Reusing what
   customers say* below.

## When and how to ask

- **Pick a moment the customer can actually judge**: a finished job, an order received and used for
  a while, a defined point in an app (the first completed project, not the first login). Write the
  rule down so it is the same for everyone.
- **Keep the ask short and neutral**: who we are, what they bought, a direct link to the real review
  page, and "we'd like your honest opinion." Nothing about stars.
- **One reminder at most**, and respect opt-outs. Drafting the ask is fine to automate; sending
  it is an attended step until the owner approves the message and the list.
- **In an app**, use the platform's own in-app review prompt and its limits (store rules are in
  [app-store](../app-store/SKILL.md)). Don't show a "do you like us?" screen first that routes only
  the yes answers to the store; that is review-gating.
- **For a small local business**, the main platform is usually its Google Business Profile. A
  printed card or a follow-up text with the review link, given to every finished job, is the honest
  version.

## Platform rules (as of 2026-10; check the current policy before relying on it)

| Platform | Incentives | Selective asking | Policy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google (Maps / Business Profile) | Not allowed | Not allowed: don't ask only happy customers or discourage negative ones | [Content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) |
| Trustpilot | Not allowed | Invitations must be neutral; no filtering by expected sentiment | [Business guidelines](https://legal.trustpilot.com/for-reviewers/guidelines-for-businesses) |
| G2 | Allowed only within its rules, disclosed and labeled | Not allowed: no positive-only eligibility | [Community guidelines](https://legal.g2.com/community-guidelines) |
| Capterra | Allowed only in its permitted programs, with disclosed terms | Real users write original reviews | [Community guidelines](https://www.capterra.com/legal/community-guidelines/) |

Never apply one platform's rule to another, and never pick a gift amount without reading the
platform's current cap and terms. When in doubt, ask with no incentive.

**United States:** the FTC's rule on consumer reviews and testimonials bans fake reviews and
incentives conditioned, openly or implicitly, on a review's sentiment. See the FTC's
[questions and answers on the rule](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
(link as of 2026-10). This file is not legal advice; a regulated or cross-border campaign goes to
the owner's lawyer.

## Answering reviews

- **Reply to the specific point**, offer a real next step, and move account details to a private
  channel. Never reveal a customer's private information to rebut them.
- **Don't invent** an investigation, a refund or a fix that nobody approved.
- **A bad review is not a fake review.** Report one only for a specific, documented rule breach,
  through the platform's own process. Never pressure a reviewer to change or remove a review.

## Reusing what customers say

A public review is not permission for every use. Before a quote goes on a site, a store listing, an
ad or an email:

- **Get the customer's permission** for that use, and check the platform's terms for reusing its
  reviews, stars or logos.
- **Keep the source**: who said it (as they agreed to be named), where, and the date. Record it in
  the evidence list of the positioning record ([positioning-record.md](positioning-record.md)).
- **Quote exactly.** Mark any cut with an ellipsis; never change the meaning or merge two people's
  words into one quote.
- **Label a hand-picked set as selected.** Don't present chosen quotes as "all reviews" or show a
  star average you didn't compute from every review.
- **Keep disclosures attached.** If the reviewer got a thank-you or has a connection to the
  business, that goes with the quote wherever it appears.
- **A testimonial doesn't prove a product claim.** "Saved me hours" from one customer can't become
  "saves hours" as a promise.

## Creators, influencers and affiliates

- **Any connection is disclosed**: payment, commission, free product, a free trial, a family or
  staff tie. A gift counts.
- **The disclosure is plain and visible**: "#ad" or a clear sentence where people actually see it
  (and said aloud in video), plus the platform's own paid-partnership label, not instead of it.
  Affiliates say "I earn a commission if you buy through this link" next to the link.
- **The brand is responsible.** Put the disclosure requirement in the brief and the agreement, and
  check that it was done.
- **No made-up claims or results**, and no creator implying they are a customer when they aren't.
  The FTC's guidance on endorsements covers US campaigns
  ([what people are asking](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking),
  as of 2026-10); other countries have their own rules.
