# Marketing models — what each one is and how to use it honestly

Reference for [SKILL.md](SKILL.md)'s diagnosis table. Each entry: what the model says, then how
it applies to an app or a website we build. Entries marked **honesty** have a misuse that the
skill's honesty check rules out.

Rewritten from the `marketing-psychology` skill in `coreyhaines31/marketingskills` (MIT, Corey
Haines). Effect sizes quoted in popular marketing writing ("losses hurt twice as much", "seven
touches") are rough rules of thumb, not measurements for our audience; treat them as
hypotheses.

## Strategy lenses (deciding what to work on)

- **Jobs to Be Done.** People hire a product to get an outcome. Lead with the outcome ("a deck
  you can use this summer"), not the spec ("pressure-treated framing").
- **Theory of constraints.** One bottleneck limits the whole funnel. If the page converts but
  nobody visits, fix traffic first.
- **Local vs global optimum.** Tuning an email subject line doesn't help if email is the wrong
  channel. Zoom out before zooming in.

## How buyers think

- **Mere exposure.** Familiar feels safe. A consistent name, look and message across the site,
  the Business Profile and social builds preference.
- **Availability.** Vivid, recent examples feel likely. A specific real project story beats a
  general claim.
- **Endowment effect.** People value what they already hold. Free trials and a working first
  result before payment make the product feel like theirs.
- **IKEA effect.** People value what they helped build. Let users configure, name or set up
  something early.
- **Zero-price effect.** Free is a category of its own, not just a low price. A free tier or
  free estimate pulls far harder than a cheap one.
- **Status-quo bias.** Change feels risky. Make switching safe: import, migration help, a
  guarantee.
- **Default effect.** Most people keep the preselected option. **Honesty:** preselect what most
  customers need, never a paid add-on or a pre-ticked opt-in.
- **Paradox of choice / Hick's law.** More options slow and stall the decision. Three plans,
  one recommended; one primary CTA; as few form fields as the job needs.
- **Goal-gradient.** Effort speeds up near the finish. Show progress in setup and checkout.
- **Zeigarnik effect.** Unfinished tasks nag. "Your profile is 80% done" pulls people back.
- **Peak-end rule.** Experiences are remembered by their best or worst moment and the end.
  Make the confirmation page, the first success and the follow-up email good.
- **Pratfall effect.** A small, real admitted limit makes the rest believable ("we're not the
  cheapest; we answer the phone").
- **Mental accounting.** The same money feels different by frame: "$3 a day" vs "$90 a month".
  **Honesty:** the reframed number must be the real total.
- **Regret aversion.** Fear of a bad choice blocks action. A guarantee, a free trial or "no
  contract" removes it, if we honor it.
- **Social proof / bandwagon.** Others choosing it signals safety. **Honesty:** only real
  reviews, counts and logos.
- **Mimetic desire.** People want what people like them want. Show real customers like the
  reader. **Honesty:** no fake waitlists.

## Persuasion

- **Reciprocity.** Give something useful first (a free tool, a checklist, a straight answer)
  before asking.
- **Authority.** Real credentials, licenses, certifications and press, shown where the doubt
  is.
- **Liking and similarity / unity.** People say yes to people like them. A real founder or crew
  story and the customer's own words help.
- **Scarcity and urgency.** Limited things seem more valuable. **Honesty:** only true limits
  (real capacity, a real deadline).
- **Loss aversion / prospect theory.** Losses loom larger than equal gains. Name the real cost
  of waiting. **Honesty:** no "don't miss out" with nothing behind it.
- **Anchoring.** The first number seen sets the scale. **Honesty:** anchor on a real
  alternative (the competitor's price, the cost of doing nothing, the higher plan).
- **Framing.** "90% of projects finish on schedule" and "10% run late" feel different. Pick the
  frame that's useful to the reader, never one that hides a fact they need.
- **Contrast.** Show the before clearly so the after is vivid. Real before/after only.

Left out on purpose (see SKILL.md): door-in-the-face pricing and decoy tiers.

## Pricing

- **Good-better-best.** Three real tiers, the middle the one most customers need and
  recommended. Every tier must be a sensible pick for someone.
- **Zero-price and mental accounting** (above) apply to pricing too.

Pricing for a client service (a contractor's estimates) is the client's call; this skill can
frame a price, not set one.

## Designing for behavior

- **Fogg behavior model.** Behavior happens when motivation, ability and a prompt meet. When
  something doesn't happen, find which of the three is missing.
- **EAST.** Make it Easy, Attractive, Social, Timely.
- **Activation energy.** The first step decides. Prefill, templates, one-field signup, a quick
  first win.

## Growth

- **Feedback loops / flywheel.** Outputs that feed inputs: happy customers → reviews → more
  visitors → more customers. Find the loop and strengthen its weakest link.
- **Compounding.** Consistent content, reviews and search presence add up; start early.
- **Switching costs.** **Honesty:** earn retention with value (accumulated history, setup that
  fits their work). Export and cancellation stay easy.
- **Exploration vs exploitation.** Keep the working channels, fund a few experiments.
