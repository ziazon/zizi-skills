# Marketing seeds — ways an app or site can attract people without ad spend

A seed list for Phase 1 when the idea is "how could this app or site attract people". Every
seed is something we can build or do ourselves, with no ad budget.

Adapted from the `free-tools` and `marketing-ideas` skills in `coreyhaines31/marketingskills`
(MIT); rewritten, keeping only the build-it-yourself ideas.

## How to use it

- Use it as one more lens in Phase 1, next to the lenses in `frameworks.md`. Pick the two or
  three seeds that fit the product and its audience; never run the whole list.
- Each seed still answers "who is this for" first, and each passes marketing's
  [Honesty check](../marketing/SKILL.md).
- A chosen seed becomes a normal idea: stress-test it in Phase 2 like any other.

## The seeds

1. **Calculator** for a number the audience has to work out anyway (a cost, a saving, a time).
2. **Grader or checker** that inspects something the audience owns and shows the problem the
   product solves.
3. **Generator** that produces something usable at once: a schedule, a starter file, a checklist.
   Anything legal (a policy, a contract) says plainly it is not legal advice.
4. **Free templates** people can use inside or outside the product.
5. **Importer from a competitor or a spreadsheet**, so switching costs an afternoon, not a week.
6. **Comparison or alternative page** with dated, fair facts and the cases where the other tool
   is the better pick. The evidence rules are in
   [positioning-record.md](../marketing/positioning-record.md).
7. **A small "powered by" link** on things users publish with the product, which they can
   switch off.
8. **A public changelog** that lists only what is live.
9. **Integrations as distribution**: a listing in another product's marketplace or directory.
10. **Shareable output**: a result, report or page users want to send on, with a quiet credit.
11. **Help pages that answer what people search**, each adding something existing answers lack.
12. **Open-source a small piece** other builders can use: a component, a library, a dataset.
13. **A browser extension or small companion utility** that is useful on its own.
14. **A try-it sandbox** with sample data and no signup.
15. **A yearly report from aggregate product data**, only with consent and nothing that
    identifies anyone.
16. **Answering where people already ask**, useful first and saying we built the thing. The
    rules are in [first-customers](../first-customers/SKILL.md).

## Picking a free tool: the scorecard

Rate each candidate low, medium or high on each line. No points; the pattern decides.

| Factor | The question | Evidence that counts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Search demand** | do people already look for this? | search suggestions, forum questions, competitor tools that get used |
| **Relevance to the product** | does using the tool show the problem the product solves? | a clear next step from the tool's result to the product |
| **Build effort** | can a first version ship in days? | one screen, one job, no accounts |
| **Maintenance** | what keeps breaking? | outside APIs, scraped data and free-text input all need upkeep and widen the attack surface ([security-and-hardening](../security-and-hardening/SKILL.md)) |
| **Lead value** | would the people who use it be the people who buy? | the tool's users match the positioning record's audience |

A good pick is high on relevance and lead value, low on maintenance, with demand evidence you
can point at. High demand with low relevance brings traffic, not customers. Don't gate the
result a visitor came for; marketing covers what may be asked in return.

## Left out on purpose

These came up in the source lists and are not seeds:

- **Paid ads, sponsorships and paid placements**: they need a budget.
- **Engagement pods** and any scheme that fakes reach or reviews.
- **Parasite SEO** (borrowing another site's authority for thin pages) and templated city or
  persona pages with no distinct content.
- **Giveaways that require follows, shares or tags.**
- **Fake-scarcity launches** and rolling "lifetime deal" deadlines.
